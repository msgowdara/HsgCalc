Chapter 1 · Genesis & Regulatory Framework

The Gold Card Scheme for Exporters is a flagship RBI initiative designed to provide creditworthy exporters with streamlined, concessional access to export credit on best terms. It was mandated in the EXIM Policy 2003-04 by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, directing RBI to work out a scheme for creditworthy exporters with good track records.

RBI Directive RBI's Gold Card Scheme (embedded in Master Circular DBOD.DIR(Exp).BC.14/04.02.002/2015-16) aims to ensure easy availability of export credit on best terms for exporters with a proven track record. In June 2019, RBI issued a specific reminder circular (RBI/2019-20/23) directing banks to strictly implement the scheme as many were not extending benefits per extant instructions.

Bank of Baroda launched its Gold Card Scheme on 29 June 2004 via circular BCC:BR:96/253 after Board approval on 24 June 2004. The scheme has been progressively updated through 8 major circulars over 19 years.

BoB Overlay BoB operates the Gold Card Scheme through the Trade, FX and Remittances Department, Baroda Corporate Centre, Mumbai. The Gold Card is issued centrally by BCC directly to the exporter under intimation to the respective Branch. The Branch must update its records upon receipt of the Gold Card advice.

The scheme has been progressively refined through 8 major circulars. Understanding this evolution is critical as exam questions frequently test the "before vs after" differences.

2004
BCC:BR:96/253 · 29.06.2004

Launch of Gold Card Scheme

Original eligibility (BBB/B+ rating), 3-year limits, BPLR minus 4% for Rupee credit, LIBOR+75bps for FC credit, 20% standby, 25-day TAT for fresh applications, 10% concession on commission/exchange

2005
BCC:INT:DFB:97/583 · 09.03.2005

Overdue Bills Benchmark Changed

Critical change: Overdue bills benchmark changed from "current year's turnover" to "previous year's turnover" — a tightening of the eligibility gate

2009
BCC:WB:DFB:101/47 · 11.09.2009

COVID-Predecessor Waiver

Temporary waiver of 10% overdue bill criterion for April 2009 – March 2010 due to global financial crisis. Exporters could qualify despite higher overdues during this period

2012
BCC:WB:DFB:104/10 · 09.02.2012

Expiry Alert — Concessions Auto-Cancelled

Alert to branches: Many Gold Cards had expired without renewal. If Gold Card expires — ALL concessions/facilities must be cancelled IMMEDIATELY. Branches directed to send renewal applications urgently

2019
BCC:BR:111/324 · 05.07.2019

Comprehensive Rationalization

Full consolidation for field-level clarity. Rating updated to BOB-6 (new model). ECGC premium for Pre-shipment made Bank's responsibility. Withdrawal criteria formally listed

2020
BCC:BR:112:628 · 20.10.2020

Major Modifications

Rating changed from BOB-6 to BOB-5. Interest concession on INR: 25 bps (no FC concession). Period extended to operating cycle + max 12 months. Standby sanctioned as per DLP. No additional collateral for standby. Withdrawal criteria detailed

2023a
BCC:BR:115:247 · 29.04.2023

Most Comprehensive Update

Multi-framework rating (BOB-5 + BBB + CMR-5). Three new eligibility routes added. Loss criterion clarified as "negative PBT." SMA-1 added as withdrawal trigger. Exception authority changed from COCC-ED to COCC-CGM. Meritorious case exception WITHDRAWN

2023b
BCC:BR:115:290 · 26.05.2023

Single-Scheme Addendum

Clarification: A borrower cannot avail benefit of more than ONE Gold Card scheme at a time. The three BoB variants (Exporters/SME/Traders Gold Card) are mutually exclusive

Chapter 2 · Eligibility Criteria (Current 2023)

The 2023 circular introduced a multi-framework rating approach replacing the single internal rating system:

Rating FrameworkMinimum ThresholdApplicable When
Internal BOBRAM RatingBOB-5 for last 2 yearsAll borrowers where External/CMR not applicable
External Agency RatingBBB for last 2 yearsWherever external rating is applicable
CIBIL MSME Rating (CMR)CMR-5 at sanction AND at issuanceWherever CMR is applicable to MSME accounts
⚠ Exam Trap — Rating Evolution 2004: BBB (new model) or B+ (old model) — qualitative/external
2019: BOB-6 (internal rating, new model)
2020: BOB-5 (internal rating — tightened!)
2023: BOB-5 + BBB (external) + CMR-5 (CIBIL MSME) — multi-framework
Note: CMR-5 must be checked at TWO points: time of sanction AND time of issuance of Gold Card.

Standard Route (a): Account must be classified as "Standard" continuously for 3 years and must not be caution-listed/blacklisted by ECGC or RBI.

The 2023 circular added three ALTERNATIVE routes for cases where standard 3-year credit history is absent:

BoB — Route (b): Current Account Entities Entities with NO credit facility with banking system but having business/current account relationship with BoB for last 2 years, routing 50% or more of export turnover through BoB for last 2 years. Eligible on sanction of fresh export credit, provided minimum rating criteria are met at sanction AND at issuance.
BoB — Route (c): New Firm (Parent/Associate) New firm established in same line of activity by parent/associate that is a BoB borrower. Conditions: (i) Common promoters with 50%+ voting rights/control in parent/associate; (ii) Parent should be availing Gold Card or satisfying Gold Card eligibility; (iii) Minimum rating criteria at sanction AND issuance.
BoB — Route: Takeover Accounts Takeover accounts can be given Gold Card if they satisfy both Gold Card criteria AND takeover norms. Rating criteria: Internal BOB-5 at sanction; External BBB for last 2 years (where applicable); CMR-5 at sanction AND at issuance (where applicable).
⚠ Exam Trap In all three alternative routes, the exporter must ALSO satisfy ALL other Gold Card eligibility criteria: not blacklisted by ECGC, no negative PBT in past 3 years, overdue bills ≤ 10% of previous year's turnover.

An exporter is NOT eligible for Gold Card if either or both of the following conditions exist:

  • Export firm making loss (negative Profit Before Tax — PBT) in any one or more of the past three years
  • Having overdue export bills as on the date of issuance of Gold Card in excess of 10% of previous financial year's turnover
BoB — Covid-19 Exception for MSMEs (Existing Borrowers Only) Net loss due to Covid-19 in FY 2020-21 and/or FY 2021-22 is overlooked IF:
✓ Entity generated cash profit (PBT + Depreciation) in BOTH FY 2020-21 and FY 2021-22
✓ Firm earned net profit in FY 2022-23
✓ All other Gold Card conditions satisfied
This relaxation is applicable only to existing borrowers.
⚠ Critical — Exception Withdrawn The earlier 'meritorious cases' exception (which permitted COCC-ED to approve Gold Card despite losses/overdue bills) has been WITHDRAWN by BCC:BR:115:247 dated 29.04.2023. Only the Covid-19 MSME exception survives.

⚠ Exam Trap — ECGC Premium Under Gold Card: Bank bears PRE-SHIPMENT ECGC premium. Under general MSME policy (non-Gold Card): pre-shipment premium is recovered FROM the borrower. Post-shipment premium is borne by Bank in both cases. This distinction — who bears pre-shipment ECGC premium — is a frequent MCQ topic.

Single Scheme Rule (BCC:BR:115:290 dated 26.05.2023): BoB operates three Gold Card variants:

  • Baroda Gold Card for Exporters — Launched 2004 (BCC:BR:96/253)
  • Baroda SME Gold Card — Launched 2008 (BCC:BR:100:98)
  • Baroda Traders Gold Card Scheme — Launched 2013 (BCC:BR:105:174)

Any additional/standby limit sanctioned to a borrower can be given under ANY ONE of these three schemes. No borrower can benefit from more than one scheme at a time.

Data Reporting to RBI: Export credit data under Gold Card Scheme is furnished to RBI in Form C on a quarterly basis — as on the last reporting Friday of each quarter (March/June/September/December). Regional Offices collect data from Branches and send to BCC for consolidation and RBI reporting.

Zonal Monitoring: Renewal/withdrawal of Gold Card Scheme must be invariably monitored at Zonal level. Zones/Regions are advised to leverage Gold Card provisions to increase export credit and garner new business.

Chapter 5 · Withdrawal Criteria (Critical for Exam)

Gold Card facility is automatically withdrawn upon occurrence of ANY of the following. Outstanding amount (including standby) is converted to regular export credit with withdrawal of concessions:

#Trigger (2023)Was it Different Before?
1Loss (negative PBT) in any financial year subsequent to issuance2020: Losses in 2+ of past 3 years
2Downgrade in External or Internal BOB rating during 2 consecutive yearsSame
3CMR rating below CMR-5 at review/renewal. If technical reason: 30-day grace; else immediate withdrawalCMR framework new in 2023
4Overdue bills > 10% of previous year's turnover for 2 consecutive quartersEarlier: "frequently remaining in excess" (imprecise)
5Adverse notice of RBI, ECGC, DOE, CBI, or any Regulatory body/Law enforcement agencySame
6Slippage to SMA-1NEW in 2023 — not in earlier versions
BoB — Exception for Continuation Despite Deficiency In exceptional/meritorious cases where Gold Card is to be continued despite deficiency:
• Proposals within COCC-CGM delegated power → permitted by COCC-CGM
• Proposals above COCC-CGM delegated power → by respective sanctioning committees
(Pre-2023, this exception was permitted by COCC-ED — now upgraded to COCC-CGM)
⚠ Three Key Changes in 2023 Withdrawal Criteria 1. Loss trigger: "2+ of past 3 years" → "ANY ONE year post-issuance"
2. New trigger: SMA-1 slippage added
3. Exception authority: COCC-ED → COCC-CGM
Chapter 6 · RBI vs BoB Comparison

The governing analytical principle for this exam: RBI sets the outer regulatory boundary; BoB's internal policy adds operational specificity — sometimes tighter, sometimes operationally defined where RBI was vague.

Interest ConcessionBetter terms than other exportersSpecifically 25 bps on INR only; NO concession on FC credit
Loss CriteriaNot making losses for past 3 yearsNegative PBT in any one or more of past 3 years (defined as negative PBT)

MonitoringBanks to monitor implementationInvariably at Zonal level

Chapter 7 · GCEMP 2025 Interface

The GCEMP (Global Credit Exposure Management Policy) 2025 specifically references the Gold Card Scheme in the context of MSME Export Credit Policy (Annex No. 3):

  • Gold Card Standby Limit = 20% of assessed MPBF to execute sudden/unanticipated export orders
  • Standby sanctioned OUTSIDE MPBF, as per DLP on basis of (Existing Total Exposure + Proposed Standby Limit), at specific customer request
  • No additional collateral security for Standby Limit
  • 25 bps concession under Gold Card is NOT available where any other interest rate concession has already been granted under DLP
  • ECGC premium for Pre-shipment credit under Gold Card is borne by Bank
  • General MSME benefit: 50% concession on processing charges (this is general MSME policy, NOT Gold Card-specific)
⚠ GCEMP 2025 Key Distinction The 50% concession on processing charges applies to ALL MSME exporter customers generally. The 10% concession on commission and exchange is Gold Card-specific. Processing charges under Gold Card are recovered on Total Exposure (including Standby) at full applicable rate — no processing charge concession under Gold Card.

⚠️Top Exam Traps
Overdue benchmark year: Always PREVIOUS year's turnover (not current year). Changed in 2005.
FC interest concession: ZERO concession on PCFC/foreign currency export credit. 25 bps applies to INR ONLY.
ECGC pre-shipment premium: Under Gold Card → Bank bears it. Under normal policy → borrower bears it.
Meritorious cases exception: WITHDRAWN in April 2023. Only Covid-19 MSME exception remains (existing borrowers only).
Withdrawal loss trigger changed: Pre-2023: losses in 2+ of past 3 years. Post-2023: ANY ONE year after issuance.
SMA-1 is NEW (2023): Earlier circulars had SMA in list but as "3 years in SMA list." 2023 changed to SMA-1 slippage directly.
Exception authority changed: COCC-ED → COCC-CGM (April 2023). Always use the current authority.
Single scheme rule (2023): Cannot avail Exporter Gold Card + SME Gold Card simultaneously. ONE at a time.
Processing charges: No concession on processing. Charged on TOTAL exposure including standby.
CMR dual checkpoint: CMR-5 required at time of SANCTION and again at time of ISSUANCE of Gold Card.
🚨Withdrawal Triggers (All 6)
1. Loss (neg. PBT) — any year post-issuance AUTO
2. Rating downgrade — 2 consecutive years AUTO
3. CMR below CMR-5 at review (30-day grace if technical) CONDITIONAL
4. Overdue >10% — 2 consecutive quarters AUTO
5. Adverse notice: RBI/ECGC/DOE/CBI/Regulator AUTO
6. SMA-1 slippage ← NEW 2023 AUTO
Any ONE trigger = automatic withdrawal. Outstanding converted to regular export credit. All concessions withdrawn.
Exception: COCC-CGM may permit continuation despite deficiency (proposals within its DLP). Above COCC-CGM: respective sanctioning committee.
