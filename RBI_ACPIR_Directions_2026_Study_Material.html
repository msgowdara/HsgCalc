Overview & Background
Why These Directions Were Issued

Indian banks were governed by the old IRACP norms (Income Recognition, Asset Classification & Provisioning) based on an incurred loss model — provisioning happened only after a default event occurred. This led to delayed recognition of losses and underestimation of credit risk. The new 2026 Directions introduce a forward-looking Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model, aligned with global standards (IFRS 9).

Chapter II – Classification as Non-Performing Asset (NPA)
Core NPA Definition – 90-Day Rule

A financial asset is classified as NPA if any of the following conditions are satisfied:

Type of FacilityNPA Trigger Condition
Term Loan / Bills Interest and/or principal overdue for more than 90 days
OD / CC (Out of Order) (i) Outstanding > sanctioned limit/DP for 90 days; (ii) No credits for 90 days; (iii) Credits insufficient to cover interest for past 90 days
OD/CC – stale stock statements Drawing permitted for 90 days based on stock/receivable statements older than 3 months
Agricultural – Short Duration Crops Overdue for 2 crop seasons (same crop season type)
Agricultural – Long Duration Crops Overdue for 1 crop season (crop duration >12 months, up to 18 months)
Securitization Liquidity facility outstanding for more than 90 days
Partial Credit Enhancement Outstanding from date of drawal for 90 days or more
Credit Cards Minimum amount due not paid within 90 days from payment due date in statement
Bonds / Debentures Interest/instalment due and unpaid for more than 90 days
Borrower-Level NPA Classification (Critical Rule)

If any one exposure to a borrower is NPA, ALL exposures to that borrower are classified as NPA. NPA classification is applied at the borrower level, not facility level.

Exception: Bills discounted under LC may NOT be classified NPA when other borrower facilities are NPA — until payment is refused or borrower defaults on LC payment.

NPA Upgrade – When Can an NPA Become Standard?
  • Only when entire arrears of interest AND principal are repaid
  • For borrowers with multiple facilities: upgrade only on repayment of all arrears across all facilities
  • Borrower and co-borrower are jointly and severally liable
Automation of Asset Classification (Annex 3)
  • Banks must flag accounts as overdue as part of day-end processes on the due date
  • NPA classification must also happen via day-end process (not delayed)
  • NPA date = calendar date for which day-end process is run
  • Example: Due date March 31 → overdue from March 31 → NPA from June 29 (90 days later)

Chapter III – Expected Credit Loss (ECL) Framework
What is ECL?

Expected Credit Loss (ECL) = the weighted average of credit losses under different scenarios, with the respective probability of each scenario as weights. It is a forward-looking, probability-weighted estimate of credit losses — neither the worst-case nor the best-case scenario.

Scope – Financial Instruments Covered Under ECL
  • Loans
  • Debt securities (not FVTPL)
  • Trade receivables
  • Lease receivables
  • Loan commitments (including undrawn)
  • Off-balance-sheet credit exposures
  • Any other financial asset with contractual right to receive cash
EXCLUDED: Investments in subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures
Fair Valuation on Transition (April 1, 2027)
  • Banks must fair value their entire loan portfolio on the transition date
  • Difference between fair value and carrying amount → adjusted against opening balance of retained earnings (NOT routed through P&L)
  • If fair value is not materially different from carrying cost → carrying cost presumed to be best evidence of fair value
Initial Recognition & Subsequent Measurement
  • Loans originated on or after April 1, 2027: Measured at fair value ± transaction costs at initial recognition; subsequently at amortised cost using EIR method
  • Loans outstanding as on March 31, 2027: Brought under EIR regime by March 31, 2030
  • POCI assets (Purchased/Originated Credit-Impaired): Use credit-adjusted EIR; only cumulative changes in lifetime ECL recognised after initial recognition

Key ECL Formula Components

PD
Probability of Default
12-month PD for Stage 1; Lifetime PD for Stage 2
LGD
Loss Given Default
Loss as % of exposure; based on collateral & recovery
EAD
Exposure at Default
Expected outstanding at the time of default

ECL = PD × LGD × EAD (present value of expected shortfall discounted at EIR)

4
The Three-Stage (Staging) Framework
Stage 1
Performing / No Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR)
  • Financial instrument has NOT experienced SICR since initial recognition, OR has low credit risk
  • Provision: 12-month ECL (ECL from defaults possible within 12 months)
  • Interest income: Recognised on gross carrying amount using EIR
  • Provisioning floor: Similar to existing standard asset norms
Stage 2
Underperforming / SICR Occurred but Not Credit-Impaired
  • Financial instrument has experienced SICR since initial recognition but is not credit-impaired
  • Provision: Lifetime ECL (ECL from all possible default events over life of instrument)
  • Interest income: Recognised on gross carrying amount using EIR
  • Provisioning floor: Minimum 5% (significant increase from existing ~0.4%)
  • Rebuttable presumption: If contractual payments are more than 30 days past due → SICR assumed (lifetime ECL)
  • For revolving facilities: Outstanding continuously > sanctioned limit/drawing power for up to 60 days → lifetime ECL
Stage 3
Credit-Impaired / NPA
  • Financial instrument is credit-impaired as at reporting date (includes all NPAs)
  • Provision: Lifetime ECL
  • Interest income: Recognised on net carrying amount (amortised cost less loss allowance) using EIR
  • Provisioning floor: Similar to existing NPA provisioning norms
  • Non-recognition of income on Stage 3 assets will NOT attract auditor qualifications
SICR – Significant Increase in Credit Risk: Key Concepts
  • SICR = Significant/material change in estimated Default Risk over remaining expected life
  • Bank must compare risk of default at reporting date vs. date of initial recognition
  • Banks must consider reasonable and supportable forward-looking information
  • SICR criteria must be documented and applied consistently
  • Collective assessment allowed for portfolios with shared credit risk characteristics
Rebuttable Presumption
More than 30 days past due = SICR assumed (Stage 2 → Lifetime ECL). Banks may rebut only with specific documented evidence that credit risk has not increased significantly.
Exemptions – Instruments NOT Requiring SICR Testing
  • SLR eligible investments
  • Direct claims on Central Government
  • Exposures fully guaranteed by Central Government
  • Exposures to Foreign Sovereigns, Foreign Central Banks, Multilateral Development Banks, BIS, and IMF — attracting 0% risk weight

These instruments also do NOT require Stage 1 ECL provisioning.

Probability-Weighted Scenarios for ECL
  • Banks must use multiple scenarios (not just base case)
  • Each scenario reflects relationship between key ECL components and macroeconomic variables
  • Probability weights determined through historical experience + expert judgement
  • Subject to Board-approved governance framework
Simplified Approach – Lease Receivables & Trade Receivables

For lease receivables and trade receivables: Loss allowances measured at Lifetime ECL always, irrespective of stage. Banks may use the "Simplified Approach" (as per Annex 2 of the Directions).

Effective Interest Rate (EIR) Method
What is EIR?

Effective Interest Rate (EIR) = the rate that exactly discounts estimated future cash flows through the expected life of the financial instrument to the gross carrying amount of the financial asset.

Key EIR Rules
  • EIR is determined at initial recognition
  • EIR calculation includes: all contractual terms (prepayment, extension options), transaction costs, fees paid/received, premiums/discounts
  • EIR calculation excludes expected credit losses
  • ECL for financial instruments originated on/after April 1, 2027 → discounted using EIR
  • For POCI assets → use Credit-adjusted EIR
  • Opening ECL (as at April 1, 2027) may use contractual interest rate as discounting factor in interim
  • All outstanding loans to migrate to EIR by March 31, 2030
EIR – Normal Assets (Stage 1 & 2)

Interest income recognised on gross carrying amount of the financial asset × EIR

EIR – Credit-Impaired Assets (Stage 3)

Interest income recognised on net carrying amount (= gross carrying amount − loss allowance) × EIR

POCI – Purchased or Originated Credit-Impaired Financial Assets
  • Assets that are credit-impaired at initial recognition
  • Use Credit-adjusted EIR (rate that discounts estimated future cash flows to amortised cost at initial recognition, already considering ECL)
  • No separate impairment allowance at initial recognition
  • Only cumulative changes in lifetime ECL (relative to estimate at initial recognition) are recognised as impairment gain/loss
7
Income Recognition Norms
Core Principle

Income on NPAs (Stage 3 assets) is NOT recognised on accrual basis. Only actual cash received may be recognised as income. This applies to interest as well as other charges like fees.

Stage / CategoryIncome Recognition Method
Stage 1 – Performing Interest on gross carrying amount using EIR; accrual basis
Stage 2 – SICR occurred Interest on gross carrying amount using EIR; accrual basis
Stage 3 – Credit-impaired / NPA Interest on net carrying amount (gross − loss allowance) using EIR; NOT on accrual basis; only actual cash receipts recognised
Standard assets (general) Accrual basis; income recognised on regular basis
Govt-guaranteed advances (overdue) Exemption from NPA classification does NOT extend to income recognition; income recognised only on cash basis if overdue
Disclosure Requirements for Income Recognition (New)
  • Separate disclosure of provisions for Stage 1 and Stage 2 assets (not netted with advances)
  • Non-recognition of income on Stage 3 assets will not attract auditor qualifications
  • Detailed credit quality tables for investments, loans, guarantees, and commitments
  • Reconciliation of loss allowances required
  • Fraud reporting requirements mandated
8
Governance, Model Risk & Operational Requirements
Governance Framework Requirements
  • ECL framework must have Board-approved governance
  • Policy, criteria, and methodology for SICR determination must be Board-approved
  • Rebuttal of the 30-day-past-due presumption requires Board or Board-committee approval
  • Probability weights in multi-scenario ECL subject to approved governance framework oversight
Model Risk Management (Chapter V Principles)
  • Broad principles for ECL model use to ensure prudence and robustness
  • Banks must maintain adequate documentation for each instance of SICR rebuttal
  • Periodic review and validation of SICR rebuttal rationale required
  • Where rebuttal basis is found inadequate → bank must cease rebuttal immediately and recognise SICR
  • Stage 2 lifetime PD must NOT be mechanically derived from Stage 1 PD parameters — must reflect higher risk profile
Data & Technology Requirements
  • Banks must build robust data systems to implement ECL framework
  • Technology-enabled automation of NPA/SMA classification (day-end process)
  • Near-real-time information sharing in Co-Lending Arrangements
  • Loan contracts must contain exact due dates, principal/interest breakup, SMA/NPA illustration

Q 1

Answer: B – The Directions were issued on April 27, 2026, but are effective from April 1, 2027. Until then, the 2025 IRACP Directions continue to govern banks.
Q 2
Under the new 2026 Directions, the provisioning approach is shifting from:
AForward-looking model to incurred loss model
BExpected credit loss model to fair value model
CIncurred loss model to Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model
DSimplified approach to complex internal rating approach
Answer: C – The old IRACP norms used an incurred loss model (provisions only after default). The 2026 Directions shift to a forward-looking ECL model (provisions based on expected future losses).
Q 3
Which of the following entities is NOT covered under the RBI (Commercial Banks – Asset Classification, Provisioning and Income Recognition) Directions, 2026?
ASmall Finance Banks
BState Bank of India
CNationalised banks (Corresponding New Banks)
DPrivate sector banking companies (excluding SFBs and Payment Banks)
Answer: A – SFBs (Small Finance Banks), Payment Banks, and Local Area Banks are EXCLUDED. SBI, nationalised banks, and private sector banks (other than excluded ones) are covered.
Q 4
A term loan account remains overdue for 91 days. The NPA date is correctly identified as:
AThe date the bank notices the overdue status
BThe 91st day from the due date as per banker's convenience
CThe calendar date for which the day-end process is run on the 91st day of overdue
DThe next quarterly reporting date
Answer: C – NPA classification must be done as part of day-end process. The NPA date is the calendar date for which the day-end process is run. If due date is March 31 → overdue from March 31 → NPA classification date is June 29 (day-end of 91st day).
Q 5
Under the ECL three-stage framework, a financial instrument classified in Stage 2 would require provisioning for:
A12-month ECL only
BLifetime ECL
C50% of Lifetime ECL
DNo provisioning required as asset is not yet impaired
Answer: B – Stage 2 = SICR occurred but not credit-impaired. Lifetime ECL must be provisioned. Stage 1 requires only 12-month ECL.
Q 6
As per the new 2026 Directions, there is a rebuttable presumption that SICR (Significant Increase in Credit Risk) has occurred when contractual payments are past due by more than:
A90 days
B60 days
C30 days
D45 days
Answer: C – More than 30 days past due creates a rebuttable presumption of SICR (Stage 2). For revolving facilities (OD/CC), SICR is presumed when outstanding remains continuously over limit for up to 60 days.
Q 7
An asset is classified as a Sub-standard NPA when it has remained as NPA for a period of:
AMore than 12 months
BMore than 18 months
CLess than or equal to 12 months
DMore than 6 months but less than 12 months
Answer: C – Sub-standard = NPA for ≤ 12 months. Doubtful = remained in sub-standard for >12 months. Loss = loss identified, considered uncollectible.
Q 8
The minimum provisioning requirement for a Doubtful asset (D2 category – 1 to 3 years in doubtful) on the secured portion is:
A25%
B40%
C100%
D20%
Answer: B – D1 (up to 1 yr): 25% secured; D2 (1-3 yrs): 40% secured; D3 (>3 yrs): 100% secured. Unsecured portion always 100% for all doubtful categories.
Q 9
In a Cash Credit account, which condition does NOT lead to "Out of Order" status?
AOutstanding continuously exceeds sanctioned limit for 90 days
BNo credits in the account for 90 days
CCredits insufficient to cover interest for previous 90 days
DAccount shows debit balance equal to (not exceeding) drawing power for 90 days
Answer: D – "Out of order" requires the outstanding to be IN EXCESS of sanctioned limit/drawing power. An account at exactly the DP level is not "out of order" on that count alone.
Q 10
Under the ECL framework, which of the following is CORRECT regarding the transition fair valuation on April 1, 2027?
ADifference between fair value and carrying amount is charged to P&L account
BDifference between fair value and carrying amount is adjusted against opening balance of retained earnings
COnly new loans originated after April 1, 2027 need to be fair valued
DFair value must be certified by an external valuer for every loan
Answer: B – The entire loan portfolio must be fair valued on April 1, 2027. Any difference between fair value and carrying amount is adjusted to opening retained earnings, NOT through P&L.
Q 11

Q 12

Q 16

A0.40%
B2.50%
C5%
D10%
Answer: C – Stage 2 minimum provisioning floor = 5% for most loan categories. This is a significant jump from the existing ~0.4% for standard assets, making Stage 2 classification financially significant for banks.
Q 17

Answer: B – Upgrade requires repayment of ENTIRE arrears of both interest and principal. For multi-facility borrowers, ALL facilities must be cleared. Both borrower and co-borrower are jointly and severally liable.
Q 18
The Effective Interest Rate (EIR) method excludes which of the following in its calculation?
ATransaction costs
BFees paid or received between parties
CExpected credit losses
DPremiums and discounts
Answer: C – EIR calculation includes transaction costs, fees, premiums/discounts, and contractual terms (prepayment options, etc.) but explicitly EXCLUDES expected credit losses. ECL is a separate component.

CSubject to Stage 1 ECL provisioning only
DTreated at par with corporate bond investments for NPI classification
Answer: A – SLR eligible investments, direct claims on Central Govt, Central Govt guaranteed exposures, and exposures to foreign sovereigns/MDBs/BIS/IMF (0% risk weight) are exempt from SICR testing and Stage 1 ECL provisioning.

Q 22

Answer: B – In Co-Lending Arrangements, borrower-level SMA/NPA classification applies to both REs. Information must be shared on near-real-time basis (latest by end of next working day).
Q 24
Which of the following is NOT included in the scope of ECL-based provisioning?
ALoan commitments including undrawn commitments
BInvestments in subsidiaries
COff-balance-sheet credit exposures
DDebt securities other than FVTPL
Answer: B – Investments in subsidiaries, associates, and joint ventures are explicitly outside the purview of ECL-based provisioning. All loans, debt securities (non-FVTPL), trade/lease receivables, loan commitments, and OBS exposures are within scope.
Q 25
The provisioning requirement for Sub-standard assets (on infrastructure loans with escrow account arrangement) is:
A15% of total outstanding
B25% of total outstanding
C20% of total outstanding
D10% of total outstanding
Answer: C – Infrastructure lending classified as sub-standard requires 20% provisioning (where an escrow account is available). Regular sub-standard: 15% secured, 25% for unsecured. This is an important nuance often tested.
